To divide is not to diminish. The cell that splits does not
become less — it becomes more. This is the paradox that
every meridian inscribes upon the globe: *separation as the
precondition of relation.*

Consider the prime meridian at Greenwich — an arbitrary line
drawn through an arbitrary building in an arbitrary empire.
And yet: without it, no longitude. Without longitude, no
navigation. Without navigation, no meeting of strangers
across the water.

The line that divides is the line that orients.
