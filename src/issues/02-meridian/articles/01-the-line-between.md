She stood at the border where the map contradicted the territory.
The cartographer had drawn a river here — she could see his ink,
the confident blue stroke — but the land held only dust.

There was a time when she would have trusted the map. When the
abstraction seemed more real than the ground beneath her feet.
That time had passed like a season she could no longer name.

· · ·

The line between hemispheres is not a wall but a hinge.
Stand on it long enough and you begin to feel the earth
turning beneath you — not as metaphor, but as the slow
undeniable rotation of a body remembering its origin.

She folded the map along its crease and placed it in the
envelope with the others. Seven maps now, each one a
correction of the last, each one more honest in its
incompleteness.

"The truest map," she wrote in the margin, "is the one
that shows you where it stops."
